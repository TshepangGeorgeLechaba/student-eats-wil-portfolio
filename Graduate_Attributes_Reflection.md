# Graduate Attributes Reflection — Student Eats

## Introduction

Student Eats is a student-focused digital marketplace concept intended to connect student food vendors with students looking for affordable meals. Through this project, the team applies multimedia design, information and communication technology (ICT), user experience design, Agile coordination, and problem-solving to a practical entrepreneurial challenge.

This reflection considers how the project relates to the four CPUT graduate attributes: Ubuntu; Knowing, Technological Capability and Foresight; Resilience and Problem-Solving Capability; and Ethical Capability and Respect.

## 1. Ubuntu

Ubuntu emphasises collaboration, mutual respect, and recognising the needs of others. Student Eats relates to this attribute through its aim of connecting student food vendors with student customers. The project considers how a digital platform could help vendors showcase their meals while helping students discover food options.

Teamwork is also important to the project. The Agile Team Charter identifies team roles, communication methods, shared working agreements, and approaches to decision-making. These arrangements provide a basis for coordinating responsibilities and respecting different contributions.

The project can demonstrate Ubuntu more strongly when the team records specific examples of collaboration, feedback, and decisions made during development. These examples should be drawn from actual team activities rather than assumed.

## 2. Knowing, Technological Capability and Foresight

This attribute relates to applying knowledge and technological skills while considering the future implications of digital solutions. Student Eats applies ICT and multimedia knowledge through the design and development of a web-based food marketplace.

The project's intended functionality includes meal discovery, browsing food listings, and supporting the ordering process. User interface and user experience decisions are important because the platform should present information clearly and make its intended tasks understandable to students.

The Trello board provides evidence of planning activities relating to research, user stories, interface design, marketplace development, and other planned features. The website and its supporting design materials can provide additional evidence of technological capability.

Future improvements should be guided by testing, user feedback, accessibility, usability, and the reliability of the ordering process. Any feature described as fully implemented should be supported by the actual website or appropriate testing evidence.

## 3. Resilience and Problem-Solving Capability

Resilience and problem-solving involve responding constructively to challenges, identifying issues, and improving work through reflection. Student Eats uses an organised project approach that includes planning, task tracking, design, development, and testing activities.

The Trello export records tasks and checklists across different project stages. These records can help the team identify completed activities, outstanding tasks, and areas requiring further attention. The Agile logbooks provide a further opportunity to document progress, difficulties, decisions, and planned actions.

Reviewing task statuses and testing results is important because a planned feature is not necessarily a completed feature. Where problems remain unresolved, they should be recorded with a realistic next step. This approach supports accountability and continuous improvement.

Specific examples of technical problems and how they were resolved should be added from the team's actual development experience.

## 4. Ethical Capability and Respect

Ethical capability involves acting responsibly, communicating honestly, and respecting the people affected by a project. Student Eats relates to this attribute because a food marketplace may involve personal information, meal descriptions, prices, and order details.

The platform should present food listings and prices clearly and should not mislead users about availability, order status, or payment confirmation. If an ordering or payment interaction is simulated, it should not be presented as a completed real-world transaction.

Responsible development also requires appropriate handling of user information, access controls where applicable, and honest reporting of testing outcomes and technical limitations. Team members should respect one another's contributions and communicate project challenges constructively.

Further evidence of ethical practice can be provided through documented design decisions, privacy considerations, testing, and the team's actual handling of user information.

## Conclusion

Student Eats provides an opportunity to apply the four CPUT graduate attributes to an entrepreneurial ICT project. Its community-focused purpose relates to Ubuntu, its digital marketplace relates to technological capability, its iterative project work relates to resilience and problem-solving, and its approach to transparent and responsible design relates to ethical capability and respect.

To strengthen this reflection, the final submission should include specific examples supported by the Agile Team Charter, Trello records, logbooks, website evidence, and actual team experiences. The reflection should accurately distinguish completed work from planned improvements.
